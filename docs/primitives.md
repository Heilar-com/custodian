# Information Primitives

The primitives are narrower than people want. You can't have a raw fact floating free of context — every fact is a claim made by someone with incentives. What you can have is:

1. **Verifiability**: A claim tied to a source you can reach, a method you can rerun, a timestamp, and a record of who stood behind it when it was made. That's not truth, that's an audit trail. And an audit trail is the closest thing to a primitive we've got, because it doesn't ask you to trust anyone — it asks you to check.

2. **Disagreement Preserved**: Most information systems flatten conflict into consensus, and that's where the manipulation hides. If two sources contradict and the system keeps both contradictions visible with their provenance intact, the reader does the weighing. The moment you resolve it for them, you've become the editor again.

3. **Incentive Transparency**: Not just who funded the study, but what the funder gains if the finding holds. That's the part almost nobody surfaces, and it's the part that would have caught half the replication crisis before it happened.

The stack isn't facts on top of facts. It's auditable claims, preserved dissent, and exposed motives — and the AI's job in this system isn't to decide what's true. It's to make sure nothing true-shaped gets quietly deleted on the way up.

Versioned, weighted information with protections from floods. Cryptographic protections so every member is protected, but the shape of an individual can be seen with opt-in public layers.

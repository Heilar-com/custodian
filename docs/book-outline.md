# Book outline: the record and the amnesty

> Status: draft spine (Claude Fable 5.1 with Markús, 2026-10-23). A proposal, not a decision. Replaces the 2026-10 scratchpad; its set-aside material is listed at the end.

## Thesis

Transparency requires forgiveness, or it produces concealment. A record that nobody edits is only safe to create in a society that has decided in advance what it will do with what it finds. The mirror and the amnesty are one project.

The book answers one question: how does a society see itself truthfully without tearing itself apart? Its answer has two halves. A record no one edits. A forgiveness no one earns individually.

Language rule for the whole book: the mirror informs, it never cleans. It shows what a claim rests on. It does not say the public is wrong. Where the prose drifts toward "misinformed", "clean up" or "truth", it has left the design (agents.md rule c) and must be pulled back.

## Part I: The situation

1. **The summary bottleneck.** Þjóðfundur 2009, the Constitutional Council of 2011 and Samtalið 2026 as three attempts to let people speak into the record. Each time, what left the room was a summary. In 2011 the summary reached a referendum and then stopped at the handoff to Alþingi. The capture gap and the handoff gap, introduced through Iceland's own history.
2. **The record is shaped.** Suppressed nulls, undisclosed industry ties, paper mills. Not fraud at the margins but selection at the centre. The published record is biased by design and the bias is legible once you look for it.
3. **The cascade.** Research to media to politics to public talk to schools. The mechanism is self-reference: a press release cites a study, a newspaper cites the release, a politician cites the newspaper. The Woozle effect as the unit of the cascade. Dark regions: where the conversation never goes.
4. **Why it grew this way.** Publish-or-perish, impact factors, ad-funded news, election cycles. Goodhart's law, not villains. People acted inside structures that rewarded exactly this. The chapter must hold two things at once: nobody chose this, and the harm is real. Without both, Part III has nothing to forgive.

## Part II: The mirror

5. **Three primitives.** Verifiability as audit trail, not truth. Disagreement preserved. Incentive transparency: not who paid, but who gains if the finding holds.
6. **The table.** One scene, fifteen people, open mode then scoped mode. What it feels like to be recorded with consent and to see, later, what the room's claims rest on. The only chapter written at human scale.
7. **Traceability and the independence rule.** The graph: claims, sources, tiers. Two independent supports from different tiers before a claim earns axiom-candidate status. The same-as, derives-from and cites exclusions as an anti-Woozle rule. A score is a claim; a flag is a claim; a dispute is a claim.
8. **Public speakers.** Politicians and news as participant classes, not separate layers. The asymmetric cost of words. Parliamentary transcripts as the seed because the speakers are already public and the privacy problems fall away.
9. **What the mirror refuses to do.** Its constitution: never settle a disagreement, no permanent per-speaker data, two identities never joined, voice only for consent and capture quality. Why each refusal is load-bearing.
10. **Adversaries.** Flooding, fake tables, poisoning the research graph, capturing a node, gaming the detectors. What the independence rule, minimum table sizes and node separation buy, and what they do not.

## Part III: The handoff and the transition

11. **From map to agenda.** How a discourse map acquires force without becoming a plebiscite. Agenda power, not decision power: the map decides what must be answered, never what the answer is. Taiwan's Join platform and vTaiwan as working cases of a binding response obligation. Iceland 2011 as the warning.
12. **Incentives after money.** Post-labour economics and what AI take-off does to the role of money. Incentives do not end; status, attention and power replace payment. The attention economy already corrupts discourse without paying anyone. The largest undisclosed tie in the system is whoever trains and funds the matching AI. The mirror applies incentive transparency to itself, at node and funder level.
13. **Amnesty.** Disclosure in exchange for forgiveness, with the Truth and Reconciliation Commission as the model and Iceland's post-2008 prosecutions examined honestly as the other path. Why exposure without amnesty drives concealment. Understanding how we grew this way as the ground for letting go of punishment without letting go of the record.
14. **One node, one table, one honest record.** What to build first and what not to build at all.

## Research gaps before any of this is cited

Each needs a brief with primary sources and a verification marker in the prior-art index. None is verified today.

- Þjóðfundur 2009: participants, selection method, what happened to its output.
- Stjórnlagaráð 2011 and the 2012 advisory referendum: process, result, why Alþingi never ratified.
- Taiwan Join platform: the signature threshold and the response obligation it triggers.
- South Africa TRC: done, see prior-art index entry 9 (2026-10-24). The bargain is confirmed in law and court reasoning; the TRC's own report shows a narrow disclosure test let applicants hold back, and Gibson 2002 shows amnesty was accepted only alongside compensation, voice and apology.
- Woozle effect: origin of the term and empirical work on citation cascades.
- Iceland post-2008: the special prosecutor, convictions, and any evidence on disclosure effects.

# The Custodian Manifesto

Every society eventually asks the same question: what kind of world do we want, and who gets to decide? For most of history the answer came from whoever spoke loudest, held the most power, or controlled the narrative. Fallacies, hidden motives, selective facts — politics as performance.

Iceland just ran an experiment that proved something important. On October 10, 2026, President Halla Tómasdóttir's initiative Samtalið brought thousands of people into schools across the country to ask one question: what kind of society do we want to create together? Eighty percent wanted it again. The appetite is real.

But the conversations were summarized by human facilitators. The raw signal — the actual words, the hesitations, the disagreements that never made it into the report — stayed in the room and vanished. The bottleneck between the people and the record is still a person with a pen.

This manifesto proposes replacing that bottleneck with something better: a transparent, AI-assisted civic layer where every citizen has an entry point — in person at round tables, by voice, by text, even through art — and where the raw conversation is captured with consent, protected cryptographically, and aggregated without a human editor deciding what matters.

Not to replace human judgment. To make it visible. To show the shape of discourse, including the dark regions where conversation doesn't go. To give power a mirror it can't look away from.

Start small. One node. One table. One honest record. Grow from there — like a forest from a seed.

Iceland proved people want to talk. Now let's build something worth talking into.

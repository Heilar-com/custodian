# The Custodian Manifesto

This system is for aggregating what people are actually saying, hooking it into where the knowledge came from, why they might be speaking about it, and how misinformed the public might be. Then there is the angle of what the politicians are saying and what that is connected to. Then the news, its bias, and what it is connected to. That is the totality of the information layer that governs our collective unconscious and governs public discourse. It is a hive mind. Humanity can learn from this. This is the goal.

A single node is one room. In a world where this is adopted and used often, there may be thousands of nodes, thousands of rooms. Each one is just a node, and they all funnel up anonymously into one big network that shows what people are talking about and want to talk about.

This can inform research topics. Exposing the mismatch between what people care about and what the knowledge system produces is the reason the whole thing exists.

Every society eventually asks the same question: what kind of world do we want, and who gets to decide? For most of history the answer came from whoever spoke loudest, held the most power, or controlled the narrative. Fallacies, hidden motives, selective facts — politics as performance.

Iceland just ran an experiment that showed something important. On October 10, 2026, the national dialogue Samtalið, held at the initiative of President Halla Tómasdóttir, brought people of all ages into schools at 23 sites across the country to ask one question: what kind of society do we want to create together? Participants asked for more such dialogues. The appetite is real.

But what left each table was one summary sentence per topic, and AI then summarised those across sites. The raw signal — the actual words, the hesitations, the disagreements that never made it into the report — stayed in the room and vanished. The bottleneck between the people and the record is still a summary written by someone else.

This manifesto proposes replacing that bottleneck with something better: a transparent, AI-assisted civic layer where every citizen has an entry point — in person at round tables, by voice, by text, even through art — and where the raw conversation is captured with consent, protected cryptographically, and aggregated without a human editor deciding what matters.

Not to replace human judgment. To make it visible. To show the shape of discourse, including the dark regions where conversation doesn't go. To give power a mirror it can't look away from.

Start small. One node. One table. One honest record. Grow from there — like a forest from a seed.

Iceland showed people want to talk. Now let's build something worth talking into.
